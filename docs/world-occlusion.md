# Placed scenery and character depth

The three regions combine clean terrain plates with 471 independently placed transparent objects: 209 in Lindenhafen, 129 in Waldruh and 133 in Nebelstadt. Each region has 32 authored silhouettes, each with separate calm day and night sets of six painted frames. `web/src/data/world-scenes.json` defines placed objects, selected silhouettes, foot anchors, widths and local collision footprints.

Characters sort by their feet. Props use their fixed ground anchor and default to `y+10.5`, just above the character depth at the same foot line. A character behind a tree, lamp or building draws beneath its transparent artwork; walking in front of the base brings the character forward. Natural sprite alpha preserves gaps between branches, under benches and through arches in every animation frame.

Each placed solid object owns local collision polygons in the canonical scene: building foundations, market counters, fountain rims, trunks, lamps, planters, benches and signs. Arches have two solid posts and an open passage. Boats do not block land routes. Animated crowns, flags and steam do not enlarge the ground footprint. Continuous segment checks prevent small bases being crossed between movement frames.

The player uses a 10-world-unit circular ground footprint in towns and interiors. All ground covered by a registered terrain tile is walkable unless a named water, cliff, rock or retaining-wall hazard or an object footprint blocks it. Grass and bare ground follow the same rule as paving. Navigation derives the outer boundary of the tile union, so tile seams do not become walls. Object footprints move, scale and mirror with their owner; deleting the owner removes its collision. Terrain masks contain only ground and terrain hazards, so moving furniture never leaves a baked-in blocker. Exact swept-circle checks preserve thin railings and provide the same clearance for held controls, click routes, snapping and path simplification. Movement stops at contact and projects remaining input along the wall tangent, including diagonal walls. Player route nodes are four units apart; legacy area audits and authored actor poses retain point feet and eight-unit nodes. Seated actors and painted doors keep their prop anchors, with a nearby reachable player approach. The scene compiler checks those approaches before saving.

`WorldScenery` uses ordinary upright Phaser sprites. Each sequence has common bounds and a shared pivot; animation changes the painted atlas frame while position, scale and orientation remain fixed. No mesh can reverse the texture's vertical coordinates. Whole-object bounds govern culling, including roof and canopy margins. Travel and time changes replace instances. Desktop caches loaded pages, while small screens release inactive region and period textures and reload them on revisit.

The export scripts preserve native alpha and use rectangular crops. They do not cut scenery out of terrain at runtime or reconstruct occlusion masks. Navigation checks cover small-base detours, story-character and object approaches, continuous movement and all resident routes. Renderer tests cover upright transforms, frame dimensions, animation, culling, reduced motion and cleanup.

Interior floors use their complete architectural outlines and wall partitions. Furniture collision rectangles use offsets relative to each prop, so furniture carries its footprint when moved. The Tiled editor stores terrain hazards in a separate Terrain collision layer and local object footprints with their owners. Clear-land scenes reject legacy street whitelist polygons and loose obstacle polygons. Regenerate masks from the canonical hazards with `node scripts/prepare-core-ground-masks.mjs`; historical street traces are no longer the navigation source.
