# Vocabulary pacing

How the game paces optional vocabulary practice. Story conversations, evidence and choices remain available in clue order regardless of restored words, review pressure or play time. Updated 2026-10-13; the numbers below come from `docs/audits/pacing.json`, regenerated by `npm run pacing`.

## The target

A learner who knows no German should be able to play for about **200 hours** and come out understanding everyday German comfortably. That works out to roughly **10 new words per hour of active play**, or about **2,000 words** over the whole journey. The course lexicon holds 3,283 lexemes, so the budget covers the A1 and A2 vocabulary and the most frequent part of B1.

## What was wrong

Before this change nothing limited intake. Measured against the course lexicon:

| Surface | Words shown | Rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First story scene (`a1-arrival`) | 75 lexemes plus 6 glossed or unmapped words | all in one conversation |
| Act I (six scenes) | 245 lexemes | about 2–3 hours of play |
| Whole story (18 scenes) | 797 lexemes plus roughly 260 names and story-only words | about 8–12 hours of play |
| Learning routes | 405 routes of 12 new words each, all open from the first minute | unlimited |
| Resident barks | any unpractised lexeme at the town's level, printed as a parcel label | one every few seconds |

That is 70 to 130 new words an hour on the story path alone, before the routes and barks. The dialogue audit only limited *story-only* words to two per scene; every A1 lexicon word was treated as already known.

## The model

The server is the only thing that introduces words. It keeps, per account, an **intake** record: credited seconds of visible play, and the ordered list of lexemes that have been **restored** (introduced). Optional learning routes, direct word drills and ambient German consult that list. Story German uses the existing translations, glosses and answer help without waiting for intake.

1. **Syllabus.** `scripts/build-syllabus.mjs` writes one deterministic introduction order for every lexeme to `web/src/data/syllabus.json` and `server/syllabus.json`. Each block of ten words takes up to eight words from the earliest story scene that still has unintroduced vocabulary (most frequent first) and fills the rest with the most frequent remaining core words, A1 before A2 before B1. The syllabus also records, per scene, the exact lexemes it uses.
2. **Budget.** A new adventure starts with 10 restored words. Every 360 seconds of credited play earns one more. Unused time never banks more than 15 words, so a long absence cannot dump an hour's worth of words at once.
3. **Heartbeat.** The client posts up to two minutes of visible, connected play per minute to `POST /api/intake/heartbeat`. The server credits no more than the wall time since the previous beat, then introduces whatever the budget allows, in syllabus order, and returns the new words. Beats are idempotent by ID.
4. **Brake.** Intake pauses, and the clock behind it stops, while **12 or more restored words have never been recognised unaided**, or while **20 or more practised words are due for review**. The rate is a ceiling, not a target that outruns the learner.
5. **Optional practice gates.** A learning route cannot be completed until all of its words are restored. A word drill (`lex-…`) on an unrestored word is rejected. Story conversations (greeting reply, evidence, intro, gate, choice), optional phrase practice and the placement sample have no vocabulary gate. Story access checks clue order, location, evidence and the saved reply receipt; it never checks a word allowance or a play-time minimum.
6. **Client.** Resident barks print only restored content words and skip routine lines that contain unrestored words. Learning routes show as locked with the number of missing words; the Word atlas lists only restored words; interior sessions skip expressions whose words are not restored. The quest log, objective dialog and witnesses always offer the next story scene, including when optional word intake is paused. The journal and routes open with **Today's words**: the restored words not yet recognised unaided, practised meaning first, then sound, then recall. A HUD chip shows the restored count and when the next word returns.
7. **Migration.** Existing adventures receive every word they have practised, plus the vocabulary of every completed or in-progress scene, in syllabus order, with no backlog of pending words.

Constants live in the syllabus file (`wordsPerHour`, `openingWords`, `bankCap`, `storyShare`, `brake`), generated from `scripts/syllabus.mjs`. Go reads them from `server/syllabus.json`; the client from `web/src/data/syllabus.json`; `scripts/syllabus.test.mjs` fails if the two copies or the checked-in audit drift from the generator.

The 3,283-lexeme course schedule at 10 words an hour reaches 2,010 words by hour 200; the authored story uses 823 lexemes and optional intake covers its last scene (`b1-atlas`) at hour 91.6, and Act I's last scene at hour 35.8. The full table, including glossed story words and words the lexicon cannot place, is in `docs/audits/pacing.json`.

Content rewrites can reduce learning load while keeping the story continuously playable:

- **Each scene introduces at most 10 new lexemes** beyond what earlier scenes and routes established (`newWordsAboveBudget` in the audit is zero). Only `a1-departure` meets that today. After the rebuild, `a1-market` (6 new), `a1-workshop` (9), `a1-bike` (8), `a1-invitation` (8), `a1-lost-found` (7) and `a1-lost-parcel` (5) are close (1 to 4 above budget); the largest overruns are `a1-arrival` (100 new), `a1-cafe` (36), `a1-timetable` (35) and `a1-room` (26), and Acts II and III have not been re-paced. The first scene must work with the 10 opening words, which means a first conversation of greetings, the player's name and one request.
- **Offer optional everyday life between scenes** (routes, interiors, review and barks), while allowing the player to continue the story immediately. Vocabulary pacing must never become a mandatory wait or practice requirement for the next conversation.
- **Story-only words glossed in the graph** (`introducedWords`) stay at two per scene, as the dialogue audit already enforces; unmapped words in the audit either become lexicon entries or are rewritten.

The audit and the syllabus make this mechanical: edit a graph, run `npm run assets`, and the table shows the optional intake coverage hour for every scene. This does not change story access.

## Running and tuning

```sh
npm run pacing                       # table of scene demand, vocabulary coverage hours and the 200-hour total
node scripts/build-syllabus.mjs      # regenerate both syllabus files after lexicon or dialogue changes
npm run test:tooling                 # drift and property checks for the syllabus and audit
go test ./...                        # heartbeat, brake, bank, optional practice gates, story access and migration (server/intake_test.go)
```

To change the pace, edit `syllabusDefaults` in `scripts/syllabus.mjs`, rebuild, and re-run the checks; `wordsPerHour` must divide 3600.

## Limits

Intake counts introduced words, not learned ones. The brake uses unaided recognition as the minimum evidence that a word has been met; retention is tracked separately by FSRS per modality. Names, numbers written as digits and story-only glosses are outside the budget. The rate and brake thresholds are design choices awaiting a learner study, not measured optima.
