# Lindenhafen — canal town

Paint a compact central-European canal town around the station parcel yard, canal market, archive quarter and working harbour. The main street joins the arrival square to the station; two narrower lanes lead to the archive and market. The canal remains visible from arrival. Every square has a reason: collecting parcels, buying food or meeting a witness. Place benches toward water, lamps along routes and stalls around the market edge.

Use a consistent 60° view from above, with upright three-quarter buildings and light from the upper left. Keep the near façade readable and the roof smaller than the visible walls. Materials are warm sandstone, weathered timber, muted sage canvas, terracotta and dark brass. Use cream `#e9d7a6`, brick `#b16c4d`, leaf `#7d9360`, water `#637f83` and shadow `#4f5b53`. The period is a fictional early railway town with hand-painted signs and no modern plastic.

An adult is 72 world units tall. Door openings are 83 × 36 units; cottage height is 190–215 units, major civic buildings 250–325, stalls 115–145, lamps 95. Plot a building and its approach before placing its painting. The inn, bakery and grocery each have their own façade and door. Keep a full person of pavement in front of each doorway. One shared native prop set gives all districts the same line weight and daylight direction.

Each 768 × 512 block needs one focal point, a route through it, at least 18 placed objects and a usable activity. Define its edge with buildings or vegetation; leave open space for people and actions. Avoid isolated benches, repeated hedge squares and decorative dead ends. Author pavement masks from the final terrain painting; use exact door and trunk footprints rather than canopy-sized collision.
