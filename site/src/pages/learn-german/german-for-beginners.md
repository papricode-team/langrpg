## Begin with a situation, not a word list

Your first goal in German can be small: introduce yourself, order a drink or ask where the station is. Pick one situation and learn a few phrases that belong together. A sentence gives a new word a job to do—and gives you something to say before you know much grammar.

You do not need to understand every word in a conversation to take part. Start by catching the greeting, the key noun and the question. Ask for repetition when you need it. That is part of using a language, not a sign that you have failed.

## Six phrases for your first conversation

| German | English | When to use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="de">Hallo!</span> | Hello! | A simple, friendly greeting. |
| <span lang="de">Ich heiße Alex.</span> | My name is Alex. | Replace Alex with your name. |
| <span lang="de">Ich lerne Deutsch.</span> | I am learning German. | Explain that you are a learner. |
| <span lang="de">Noch einmal, bitte.</span> | Once more, please. | Ask for a repetition. |
| <span lang="de">Langsamer, bitte.</span> | More slowly, please. | Ask someone to slow down. |
| <span lang="de">Danke schön!</span> | Thank you very much! | Thank someone for their help. |

Say each line aloud, then cover the German and try to recall it from the English. Change the name in the introduction. Imagine a real person waiting for your reply: the goal is to retrieve a useful sentence, not just recognise it on the page.

## A gentle first-week plan

**Day 1: introduce yourself.** Learn the greeting and name sentence above. Add <span lang="de">Ich komme aus …</span> (“I come from …”) with your country. Some country names need an article; look up the complete phrase for yours.

**Day 2: listen and repeat.** Find a short beginner recording with a transcript. Listen once without reading, once while reading and once after covering the text. Copy the rhythm, without expecting to sound perfect.

**Day 3: order one thing.** Practise <span lang="de">Einen Kaffee, bitte.</span> Then change coffee to tea: <span lang="de">Einen Tee, bitte.</span> Our [café guide](/learn-german/ordering-coffee-in-german/) takes you through the rest of the exchange.

**Day 4: meet noun genders.** Learn a few nouns with their articles: <span lang="de">der Bahnhof</span>, <span lang="de">die Straße</span>, <span lang="de">das Café</span>. Treat each article and noun as a pair. Start with the [der, die, das guide](/learn-german/der-die-das/).

**Day 5: ask a question.** Use <span lang="de">Wo ist …?</span> (“Where is …?”). Point to places on a map and make three questions. Add <span lang="de">Entschuldigung</span> (“Excuse me”) before asking a stranger.

**Day 6: try a small task.** Read a simple menu, follow a short dialogue or play a scene in German. Notice a phrase you have already learned. If the material is overwhelming, shorten the task or turn on support.

**Day 7: return without looking.** Try your introduction and café order from memory. Keep the phrases you can use, revisit the ones you cannot and choose next week’s situation.

## Three habits worth keeping

Learn nouns with their articles. Learn verbs inside short sentences. Give listening its own time rather than assuming reading practice will cover it. These habits keep pronunciation, grammar and meaning connected from the beginning.

Keep the first routine short enough to repeat. Ten focused minutes is a useful starting experiment, not a universal prescription. Some days you will have more time; on others, recalling three phrases is still a useful visit to the language.

## Try it before you go

Introduce yourself and tell someone you are learning German. Then ask them to repeat something more slowly. Can you do all three without looking above?
