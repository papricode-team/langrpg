## Start with one polite sentence

<span lang="de">Einen Kaffee, bitte.</span> means “A coffee, please.” It is short, natural and enough to get started. For a fuller request, use <span lang="de">Ich hätte gern einen Kaffee.</span> (“I would like a coffee.”)

You may hear <span lang="de">Was möchten Sie?</span> (“What would you like?”) or <span lang="de">Was darf es sein?</span> (“What can I get you?”). You do not need to translate every word before answering. Recognising that it is your turn to order is enough.

## A café conversation you can reuse

| Speaker | German | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Café owner | <span lang="de">Guten Morgen! Was möchten Sie?</span> | Good morning! What would you like? |
| You | <span lang="de">Einen Kaffee, bitte.</span> | A coffee, please. |
| Café owner | <span lang="de">Mit Milch?</span> | With milk? |
| You | <span lang="de">Ja, bitte.</span> | Yes, please. |
| Café owner | <span lang="de">Sonst noch etwas?</span> | Anything else? |
| You | <span lang="de">Nein, danke.</span> | No, thank you. |

If you want black coffee, try <span lang="de">Ohne Milch, bitte.</span> (“Without milk, please.”) If you need a moment, say <span lang="de">Einen Moment, bitte.</span> It buys you time to read the menu and decide.

## Why is it einen Kaffee?

The dictionary form is <span lang="de">der Kaffee</span>: Kaffee is masculine. In an order such as <span lang="de">Ich möchte einen Kaffee</span>, the coffee is the direct object. The masculine indefinite article changes from <span lang="de">ein</span> to <span lang="de">einen</span> in the accusative case.

The short version leaves out <span lang="de">Ich möchte</span>, but the form stays <span lang="de">einen Kaffee</span>. You can use this phrase comfortably before you have mastered every case. Learn the chunk first; use the grammar to understand the pattern when you are ready.

Compare these requests:

- <span lang="de">Einen Tee, bitte.</span> — A tea, please. (<span lang="de">der Tee</span>)
- <span lang="de">Eine heiße Schokolade, bitte.</span> — A hot chocolate, please. (<span lang="de">die Schokolade</span>)
- <span lang="de">Ein Wasser, bitte.</span> — A water, please. (<span lang="de">das Wasser</span>)

Notice that the feminine and neuter indefinite articles do not change between nominative and accusative. Our [German articles guide](/learn-german/der-die-das/) explains the starting forms.

## Staying, taking away and paying

<span lang="de">Hier oder zum Mitnehmen?</span> asks whether you want to stay or take your order away. Answer <span lang="de">Hier, bitte</span> (“Here, please”) or <span lang="de">Zum Mitnehmen, bitte</span> (“To take away, please”).

To ask for the bill, say <span lang="de">Die Rechnung, bitte.</span> To ask about card payment, use <span lang="de">Kann ich mit Karte bezahlen?</span> (“Can I pay by card?”). You may hear <span lang="de">Zusammen oder getrennt?</span> (“Together or separately?”) when paying with another person.

The café’s payment process may differ: some take payment at the counter, others at the table. Let the situation guide you and ask when you are unsure. The language gives you options; it does not prescribe a single ritual.

## Your turn at the counter

Order a tea to take away. Decline anything else. Ask whether you can pay by card. Try the three replies before opening the answer.
